| 16 | Submission to SECU on its study of Bill C-22 | Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada — https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/opc-actions-and-decisions/advice-to-parliament/2026/parl_sub_260521/ | 2026-05-21 | Primary statement of the Commissioner's identified privacy risks |
| 17 | Major VPN provider says it could leave Canada over lawful access bill | The Canadian Press / BNN Bloomberg — https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/business/2026/05/15/major-vpn-provider-says-it-could-leave-canada-over-lawful-access-bill/ | 2026-05-15 | Public Safety spokesperson Simon Lafortune: government rejects the backdoor characterisation and says legal authorization such as a court warrant would still be required |

**Why this needs care.** Source 17 is the *government's* position, not an
undisputed description of the bill. Sources 6 and 15 record that, as
introduced, an officer with reasonable grounds to suspect could demand that a
telecommunications service provider confirm whether a person is a subscriber,
without prior judicial authorization. Any brochure sentence asserting that a
warrant "remains necessary" must be attributed to the government rather than
stated as fact.
