| # | Source | Publisher | Date | Supports |

| 16 | Submission to SECU on its study of Bill C-22 | Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada — https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/opc-actions-and-decisions/advice-to-parliament/2026/parl_sub_260521/ | 2026-05-21 | Primary statement of the Commissioner's identified privacy risks |
| 17 | Major VPN provider says it could leave Canada over lawful access bill | The Canadian Press / BNN Bloomberg — https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/business/2026/05/15/major-vpn-provider-says-it-could-leave-canada-over-lawful-access-bill/ | 2026-05-15 | Public Safety spokesperson Simon Lafortune: government rejects the backdoor characterisation and says legal authorization such as a court warrant would still be required |

**Why this needs care.** Source 17 is the *government's* position, not an
undisputed description of the bill. Sources 6 and 15 record that, as
introduced, an officer with reasonable grounds to suspect could demand that a
telecommunications service provider confirm whether a person is a subscriber,
without prior judicial authorization. Any brochure sentence asserting that a
warrant "remains necessary" must be attributed to the government rather than
stated as fact.

**Open item — the September 24 letter.** Sources 22 and 23 almost certainly
describe the same letter (same date, same signatories named), but neither
publishes the signatory list and the letter text itself was not retrieved.
"Some twenty" on page 4 rests on source 22. Before the next printing, fetch
the letter from the Chamber of Progress and confirm the count and that
NordVPN, Windscribe and ExpressVPN signed it.

**Signal — removed in 1.1.0.** The Signal sentence (source 20) was cut from
page 4 to make room for the September letter, since Signal is a messaging
service rather than a VPN. Source 20 is retained for the Windscribe claim.

**Unsupported claim — removed.** The earlier draft stated that Windscribe's
public warrant canary declares it has not been compelled to log user activity,
build a backdoor, weaken its service or hand over encryption keys, and that
Windscribe said its service would continue without changes to its logging or
privacy policies. No source located supports either statement, and the second
materially understates what Windscribe actually said. Both were removed. If a
warrant-canary claim is wanted, cite the canary page itself with a retrieval
date. As of 2026-09-29 TechRadar (source 23, related coverage) reports
Windscribe saying there will be no changes to its logging or privacy
policies, so the statement is now sourced; it was still left out because on
its own it understates Windscribe's position.

**Source declined.** knowmina.com describes Bill C-22 as the "Online Safety
and Digital Intelligence Act", dates it to February 2026 and publishes a
retention-period table. None of this matches the parliamentary record. Not
used.
